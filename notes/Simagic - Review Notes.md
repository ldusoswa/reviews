## Simagic P500 Pedals

### Key Specs & Sensors
- Dual-sensor brake: 100 kg load cell for short-travel race feel + Hall angle sensor option for longer-travel road-car style; Hall sensors on throttle/clutch.
- Resolution: 12-bit (4096). Control box included.
- Materials: automotive-grade die-cast aluminum alloy, high-precision CNC; total footprint ~330.4 x 355.1 x 209.3 mm; weight ~3.8 kg.

### Adjustability & Ergonomics
- Lateral pedal spacing adjustment via multiple mounting holes.
- Pedal face displacement and angle adjustments to fit foot size and seating position.
- Swappable springs and elastomers; quick-change mechanism for springs/elastomers; adjustable travel, damping, linearity, brake force, and deadzones.
- Mounting: seat/floor assemblies and wall brackets included; supports rig or floor mounting; upgrade path from 2-pedal to 3-pedal.

### Feel & Options
- Smooth, precise inputs with load-cell brake for immersion and Hall sensors for consistency.
- Optional haptics: P-HPR Neo (rumble motors, lower inertia) and P-HPR (linear motor) to emulate ABS/TC/clutch bite.

### Connectivity & Software
- SimPro Manager for tuning (vibration, amplitude, mapping); plug-and-play pedal control box.

### Compatibility Notes
- Mounts via provided hardware; upgradeable kit from dual to triple pedal set.

### First Impressions (Video Notes)
- Packaging includes light plastic base plate with integrated control box, close-together mounts, carpet grips; Logitech-like form factor; cables route cleanly with gaps; comes with stickers, T-slot nuts, springs/elastomer kit, tools.
- Pedal angle highly adjustable (desk-friendly upright to rig-friendly laid back); pedal face lateral offset holes allow finer spacing than base slots; face angle/placement adjustments help heel/toe but still cramped when brake fully pressed.
- Default brake: light, limited progression; similar feel to Logitech G Pro/SRP Lite; better than SRP Lite perf kit; softer than CSL Elite V2. Swapping to heavier spring (black) gives two-stage feel (travel to ~70-80%, then load cell), requires recalibration (preload raises baseline %). Load-cell signal stable (no drop-off).
- Throttle/clutch: light but predictable; fine for desk/casual drift; clutch haptics not very useful.
- Haptics: P-HPR naming confusing; rumble motors work, default gearshift effect too long until tuned; SimPro Manager haptics UI fussy (per-effect sliders, slow UI), but ABS/TC/slip feedback useful; SimHub also supported.
- Value: < $300 (2-pedal), ~ $350 with clutch; haptics lift to ~$400; very light set good for motion rigs; not world-beaters but easy to drive quickly in default form; good entry-level load cell option.

## Alpha Evo Pro Firmware Update Revisit (SimPro 3)

### Context & Motivation
- **Date**: March 2026
- **Firmware**: SimPro 3 (significant update from previous version)
- **Reason for Revisit**: Alpha Evo Ultra impressed at 18 Nm, wanted to see if firmware improved Pro
- **Comparison Point**: Same car, same track, same sim as always used for testing
- **Status**: Simagic sending replacement wheelbase (not received yet)
- **Concern**: "If there's something wrong with my base because so many people are saying the Evo Pro, they can barely hold on to it at 100%"

### Assetto Corsa Testing - DISAPPOINTING

#### Initial Test at 9 Nm (Default Profile)
- **Default Profile**: Sets to 9 Nm torque
- **First Impression**: "This feels very light"
- **Comparison to Ultra**: "I tried the Alpha U on 9 Newton meters as well, and it felt definitely felt more than this"
- **Verdict**: "This doesn't feel like 9 Newton meters at all"
- **Detail Quality**: "Doesn't feel bad" - detail is there
- **Power Issue**: "Doesn't feel like there's a lot of power on tap here"
- **Range**: "The way it delivers the range is good"

#### Test at 18 Nm (100%)
- **Settings**: 18 Nm, 100% in-game, default profile settings
- **Immediate Issue**: "I can feel that kind of notchiness, that graininess. I did not have that with the Alpha Evo Ultra"
- **Power Assessment**: "Definitely feels a lot heavier" but "it's not tasking"
- **Major Concern**: "There must be something wrong with my base. There just must be"
- **Comparison to Other Bases**: 
  - "If this was the Asetek Invicta or the Simucube SC2 Sport, I wouldn't be able to hold on to that in the exact same car, same simulator"
  - Can drive one-handed at 100%
  - "I shouldn't be able to just drive this one-handed like this"
- **Force Feedback Quality**: "Feels excellent. There's nothing wrong with the force feedback. I'm just missing power"
- **Perceived Power**: "If someone told me this was like a 14 Newton meter wheelbase, I'd be like, 'Yeah, okay. I believe it.' 18 Newton meters... I feel like there's power missing"

#### Settings Experimentation
- **No High Torque Mode**: Unlike the Ultra, Pro doesn't have this option
- **Tried Adjustments**:
  - Details way up (created loads of rumbling)
  - Rotation speed adjustments
  - Mechanical damper down
  - Friction down
  - Slew rate control off (for rough FFB)
  - Prediction level off
- **Result**: "It feels excellent. There's nothing wrong with the force feedback. I'm just missing power"

#### Comparison to Alpha Evo Ultra
- **Critical Finding**: "The Ultra at 18 Nm. So when I set the ultra, which is a 28 new meter capable, when I set that to 18, it is far more powerful than this thing"
- **Same Firmware**: Both using SimPro 3
- **Conclusion**: "There is something wrong there in Assetto Corsa"

#### Raw Range Test (No Filters)
- **Method**: "Take all those filters, dampers, friction, everything. Take everything out and try to feel that raw range"
- **Purpose**: "It's not a great driving experience, but it tells you a lot about the wheelbase and its capabilities"
- **Result**: Still lacking power and range

### iRacing Testing - IMPROVED

#### Initial Test at Sport Level (9 Nm)
- **In-Game Setting**: 10.3 Nm
- **Impression**: "Detail is there. The detail feels very good"
- **Power**: "Definitely feels... more powerful than the sport even at these levels"
- **Detail Quality**: "Good amount of detail"
- **Slide Detection**: "I was able to feel it and not necessarily predict it, but I was able to feel it happening"

- **Result**: "Definitely a lot more force here"
- **Issues Detected**:
  - Mechanical damper "a bit rotten"
  - Mechanical friction "a little bit much"
- **After Adjustments**: "Feels a little bit more natural now... feels a little bit more like the tires are touching the road"
- **Auto Setting**: Recommended 4.6 Nm (very light)
- **Optimal Setting**: Around 11-12 Nm in-game
- **Verdict**: "That's very nice. That's really nice"

#### Power Assessment in iRacing
